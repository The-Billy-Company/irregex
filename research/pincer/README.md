# Pincer — anchor selection for the two-probe literal filter

A literal scan's throughput is decided by **which two needle offsets** the
vector unit compares, not by the instruction sequence that compares them. The
kernel's 64-byte two-probe block filter runs at 30–50 GB/s when its anchors are
chosen well and at 2.4 GB/s when they are not — a 20× spread with the
instruction sequence held fixed.

The standard way to choose those offsets, shared by the Rust `memchr` crate's
rare-byte heuristic and by `rarity.zig` here, ranks bytes by **individual**
frequency. That prices a two-position conjunction as `P(a)·P(b)` — it assumes
the probes are independent. Text is the worst case for that assumption: the
correlated unit is the word, so correlation is strongest at exactly the short
distances a needle offers. A unigram-minimising selector therefore drifts toward
correlated pairs, and the conjunction degenerates toward a single-byte filter.

Here that drift was a collapse, because `rarity.zig` clamps its density at 255 and
20 of 26 lowercase letters tie there. With every byte tied, the old strict `<`
tie-break never displaced its initialisers and returned `o1=0, o2=1` — the
adjacent, maximally-correlated pair — for **122 of 177** code needles and **78 of
90** prose needles. The shipped selector was consequently *worse than the fixed
first+last it replaced*, on every summary statistic, in both regimes.

**Status.** Both halves of the conjunction are repaired. The decision now lives in
one module, `kernel/scan/anchor.zig`, whose tie-break resolves toward the widest
separation instead of the adjacent pair; and `rarity.zig` carries its full range as
an unclamped `[256]u16`, regenerated by a checked-in census with zero rank
inversions and no lowercase letter at the ceiling. The defect signature that
started this — a degenerate needle running **41% slower** than a length-matched
control finding 19× more matches — now measures **1% faster**, the correct
direction (§10.1 of [`PROOF.md`](PROOF.md)).

The two repairs are **redundant rather than additive**, and §10.2 prices all four
corners to show it: the table was the larger defect, and the separation tie-break
is what keeps the failure graceful if a future census ever re-introduces ties. Read
§10.3 before trusting any single trap/control ratio — a cold page cache moves it far
enough to imitate the defect.

The per-buffer calibrating selector is **built, tested, and now shipped** in
`kernel/scan/calibrate.zig`, and reaches 1.03–1.04× of the best-possible pair
across code, prose, and a heterogeneous buffer — where the static table is
1.39–2.21× — for 0.19% of a scan. §7.2 of [`PROOF.md`](PROOF.md) said the missing
piece was a **per-scan plan** rather than a bigger gate, and that is what was built:
`simd.Plan` is the decision as a value, `simd.planOn` is the document-grain mint,
and three seams carry it to every literal scan — `simd.Gate.on` for the
required-literal gate, `Emitter.lit_plan` for the hit-jumping sweeps, and
`PikeScratch.litPlan` for the span walks.

Two things §7.2 did not anticipate, both recorded in `calibrate.zig` rather than
shipped as defects. Adopting the sample's favourite *unconditionally* is a measured
CPU tax, not a win: the shipped table already picks the oracle pair on 80/177 code
needles, and swapping off it also forfeits the single-probe block shape. So the
integration is an **improvement test** (`refine`) with the incumbent priced on the
same sample. And a purely relative accept margin is a **winner's curse** — the
argmin of up to 120 noisy estimates of one density sits several sigma below the
truth — so the margin is the larger of 12.5% and four standard deviations.

Measured end-to-end on a 200 MB buffer whose alphabet is the statically-rare bytes,
over three needles whose locally-rarest byte the shipped table ranks common:
**6.9–8.0× less CPU** on the whole-buffer literal modes (7.8–8.3× for `--json`, 7.0×
for `-q`), 17.6–17.9× on the kernel sweep underneath (4.09 M block survivors down to
34–42), median 1.002× where the table was already right, and byte-identical output
across 411/411 ripgrep parity cases and 420 in-binary differentials.

Also still ahead: a pair-aware policy for the residual ~1.5× to oracle.

Pincer names the repair: choose the **pair**, jointly, from statistics that can
see the correlation — and, since no shippable static table can (measured, §6),
read those statistics off the buffer being searched.

## What was measured

| | shipped | unclamped census (512 B) | joint skip-gram (4 MB) | **stratified calibration (no table)** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| code — survivors vs oracle | 4.61× | 1.49× | 1.01× | **1.01×** |
| prose — survivors vs oracle | 6.97× | 1.76× | 1.00× | **1.00×** |
| code — wall clock | 18.1 GB/s | 31.3 GB/s (1.73×) | 35.5 GB/s (1.96×) | — |
| prose — wall clock | 13.1 GB/s | 26.8 GB/s (2.06×) | 33.4 GB/s (2.56×) | — |
| static table | 256 B | 512 B | 4 MB | none |
| distribution assumption | code, clamped | code | the fitted corpus | none |

Confirmed in the shipped binary without a patch: on a single 1.7 GB file,
`stepSec` (7 bytes, 464 true hits) runs at 18.8 GB/s while `pgxpool` — same
length, **19× more true matches to verify** — runs at 26.5 GB/s. More real work,
41% less time; the difference is admitted false candidates and nothing else.

The design the measurements point to is not a better table. It is **stratified
self-calibration**: price the candidate pairs on a sample spread in small
windows across the buffer, then scan with the winner. At a 1 MB budget that
reaches the oracle in both regimes with no static table and no distribution
assumption — so base64 blobs, minified bundles, and prose are handled by
construction rather than by a table that describes none of them. At a **64 KB**
budget it costs **0.2% of the scan** and still lands at 1.11×/1.08× against the
shipped 4.61×/6.97×, so nearly the whole win is available for almost nothing.

Two obvious compactions of the static joint table were measured and **both lose
to a 512-byte unigram table**, for opposite and instructive reasons — which is
what ruled the static approach out and pointed at calibration (`PROOF.md` §6).

## The code

Both repairs and the calibrating selector are integrated. The defect lived at
`src/kernel/scan/rarity.zig` (the clamp) and
`src/kernel/scan/simd.zig::indexOfPos` (the tie-break and the two-probe loop);
`src/kernel/scan/anchor.zig` now owns the static decision, `calibrate.zig` the
per-buffer one, and `simd.zig`'s `Plan` / `planOn` / `Gate.on` the seam between
them. Reproduction harnesses: `spikes/anchor-joint-rarity/` for the defect,
`spikes/anchor-plan/` for the plan (`sweep.zig` is the kernel ground truth,
`probe.zig` reports per-needle headroom) — see `TESTING.md`.

## What is ours, after refereeing

A dated adversarial prior-art pass (2026-07-29) leaves less novelty than the
measurements might suggest, and the honest split matters:

- **the defect** — the clamp, the tie-break, the `0:1` collapse, and the
  Certificate blind spot that hid them — is ours;
- **the diagnosis** was published by [Richard
  Startin](https://richardstartin.github.io/posts/heuristics-for-substring-search)
  in 2018, in this exact domain, with real-corpus bigram tables. We rediscovered
  it;
- **self-calibration is killed as a claim** — zoekt already picks its two probes
  from live posting-list lengths, Optimal Seed Solver from the real reference
  index, and `memchr` ships a trait for exactly this substitution. We should
  adopt it *because* it is known-good; what the measurements add is the price
  (0.2%) that the incumbent's "far too expensive" assumed without measuring;
- **narrowly unclaimed:** minimising `P(X_i = a ∧ X_{i+d} = b)` over concrete
  byte values at a specific gap, per-needle, as the direct objective for a
  **conjunctive** filter. Buhler–Keich–Sun (2003) and iedera already choose
  filter positions under an empirical Markov background, so the general move is
  23 years old.

Patent coverage in that pass is weak, so nothing here is assertable externally
yet. Details, including the six specific findings that would kill claim 1, are in
`PRIOR_ART.md`.

## Status

**Integrated, and measured end-to-end.** The graduation path `TESTING.md` laid out
was followed in order: widen the clamp first (a pure dynamic-range fix worth
1.73×/2.06× on its own), then hoist the decision into a value, then calibrate it per
document. Selectivity is exact; wall clock and the end-to-end CPU ratios are Apple
M4 only, so the hardware-independent re-run on the Anvil box is still owed.

One finding is worth acting on independently of the rest: the Certificate's
`literal-rare` rung probes `pgxpool`, whose first two bytes happen to form a
rare digraph, which is why a 20× defect sat in a benchmarked hot path
undetected. That is a probe-selection blind spot, not a measurement error.
