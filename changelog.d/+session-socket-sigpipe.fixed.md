The resident session's socket writes can no longer kill the daemon (or a client) with SIGPIPE when the peer half-closes mid-write: `protocol.writeAll` now issues a no-signal send (Linux `MSG_NOSIGNAL`; Darwin/BSD `SO_NOSIGPIPE` armed idempotently on the fd), so a broken connection surfaces as EPIPE and costs one dropped connection instead of a fatal signal that takes down the whole process. The CLI's stdout SIGPIPE (the `gist | head` early exit) is deliberately left intact.
